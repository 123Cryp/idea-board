# Lenses

Three independent lenses. Score each one separately before looking at the others.

## 1. Offer

Question: is the deal so good that a stranger says yes without being talked into it?

Check four things:

- Outcome: how big and how clear is the result the buyer gets?
- Proof: how likely is the buyer to believe it will work for them?
- Wait: how long until they see the first result?
- Effort: how much work, money or risk must the buyer take on?

Score guide:

- 5: big clear outcome, strong proof, fast result, almost no effort for the buyer.
- 3: the outcome is real but proof or speed is weak.
- 1: the buyer must take a leap of faith with no proof.

Typical failures: a vague promise, a result that arrives months later, a product that adds work for the buyer.

## 2. Monopoly

Question: if it works, why can't ten others copy it next month?

Check:

- Secret: what does the builder know or have that others do not?
- Advantage that grows: does it get better or cheaper with each customer?
- Lock-in: what does a customer lose by leaving?
- Niche: is there a small market the builder can own completely before widening?

Score guide:

- 5: a real secret or a growing advantage, in a niche the builder can dominate.
- 3: some edge, but copyable in weeks.
- 1: a thin copy of what exists, competing on price.

Typical failures: "we do it better", a big market with no wedge, an edge that is only effort.

## 3. Focus

Question: can the builder ship the smallest useful version in weeks, and is it simple enough to explain in one sentence?

Check:

- One user, one problem, one action.
- Size of the smallest version that gives the buyer the outcome.
- What can be cut without hurting the outcome?
- Does the builder's time, money and skill actually fit the plan?

Score guide:

- 5: one clear user and problem, first version fits in weeks of part-time work.
- 3: good core, but too many features or users.
- 1: needs a platform, a team or a large budget before the first customer.

Typical failures: building everything for everyone, polish before a single paying user, a plan that needs more hours than the builder has.

## Cross-checks

After scoring, look for conflicts:

- High Offer, low Focus: great promise, wrong size. Cut scope.
- High Monopoly, low Offer: defensible but nobody wants it. Re-test the buyer.
- High Focus, low Monopoly: easy to build and easy to copy. Find the wedge or expect a race to the bottom.
